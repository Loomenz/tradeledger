Cleaning business startup costs: what you actually need

You can start a residential cleaning business lean. Here's what to buy first, what can wait, and how to keep track of it from day one.

Buy first

  • Supplies: all-purpose cleaner, glass cleaner, disinfectant, bathroom cleaner, microfiber cloths, sponges, gloves and trash bags.
  • Basic equipment: a good vacuum, a mop and bucket, a duster, a scrub brush set and a carry caddy.
  • Liability insurance: clients will ask, and one accident can cost more than a year of premiums.
  • Business registration: check your city and state for business license rules.

Can wait until you have clients

  • Bonding, unless clients require it.
  • Branded uniforms and car magnets.
  • Booking and scheduling software.
  • Commercial-grade equipment.

Low-cost marketing that works

  • A free Google Business Profile.
  • Asking every happy client for a review and a referral.
  • Local Facebook and Nextdoor groups.
  • Door hangers in the neighborhoods you already clean.

Track every dollar from day one

Every startup purchase is a business expense, so keep the receipts. Log them in a spreadsheet as you go, and you'll know exactly when you've earned your startup costs back. Read tax deductions cleaners often miss next.
