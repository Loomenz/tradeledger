How much to charge for house cleaning

The short version: decide what you want to earn per hour, add your costs, then add a margin. Here's how to do it without guessing.

1. Pick your pricing style

StyleBest forWatch out for
HourlyNew cleaners, deep cleans, unknown homesClients watch the clock; you earn less as you get faster
Flat rate per visitRecurring clientsNeed to time the first visit to price it right
Per room or square footQuoting over the phoneMessy homes take longer than the size suggests

Most established cleaners quote a flat rate, but they work it out from hours behind the scenes.

2. Start from the pay you want

Pick a target hourly pay for yourself. Multiply by the hours the job will take and the number of cleaners.

3. Add the costs people forget

  • Supplies: often $5 to $15 per standard home.
  • Driving: round-trip miles times a per-mile cost.
  • Overhead: insurance, phone, software and ads, often 10 to 15% on top.
  • Taxes: self-employed people pay income tax and self-employment tax, so set some of every payment aside.

4. Add a margin and round up

Add 10 to 20% so the business can grow, then round up to a clean number. Our free price calculator does all of this for you.

5. Check it after the job

Time every job for the first month and compare what you charged with what you actually earned per hour. Raise prices for the jobs that come in under target.
